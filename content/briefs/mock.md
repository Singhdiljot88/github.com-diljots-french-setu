# Mock exam brief

src/content/mock-schema.ts (Mock). Validate with `npx tsx scripts/validate-content.ts content/mocks/<id>.json`

`disclaimer`: "Original practice material written for French Setu. Not official TEF material, not recalled or leaked exam content. Results are raw practice scores, not TEF scores, CEFR levels or NCLC levels."

Verified official structure to imitate (source: Le français des affaires / CCI Paris Île-de-France sample papers):

READING (60 min, 40 questions, one correct answer among 4 options, free navigation):
- section "everyday" — Q1–7: seven short everyday documents (notices, signs, ads, messages, labels), 1 question each.
- section "gaps" — Q8–17: gap-filled sentences and a gap-filled text (use "___" in the document text; each question asks which option fits a numbered gap). Grammar/vocabulary/connectors in context.
- section "quick" — Q18–22: quick reading of varied documents including at least one chart or table rendered in plain text.
- section "admin" — Q23–32: administrative and professional documents (workplace emails, rules, contracts, official letters).
- section "press" — Q33–40: press articles (2–3 articles of 250–400 words), literal, inference and author-viewpoint questions.

LISTENING (40 min, 40 questions, each recording played ONCE, no going back):
- section "pictures" — Q1–4: short conversations. The real test uses drawings; we cannot, so each option is a short description of a scene in French (e.g. "Un homme achète des billets au guichet."). 1 question per item.
- section "messages" — Q5–20: public announcements, voicemails (répondeur), street interviews (micro-trottoir); 1 question per item mostly.
- section "radio" — Q21–30: radio interviews and a radio report (longer items with 2–4 questions each).
- section "everyday" — Q31–40: various everyday audio documents, increasing difficulty.
Audio is generated by text-to-speech from `lines` (voices f1, m1, f2, m2, narrator), so write natural spoken French that sounds right when read aloud, at natural speed register (contractions in speech are fine in street interviews). Questions are in French (q.fr) with an English gloss (q.en). Every question must be answerable from the lines; put the exact supporting quote in `evidence`. Explanations in English and Hindi.

WRITING: a.articleStart = the first 3–5 sentences of an original fait divers (news-in-brief) to continue in 80+ words; a.instruction bilingual; minWords 80. b.statement = one opinion sentence "taken from a newspaper" (original); b.instruction asks for a letter to the newspaper giving and justifying an opinion with at least three arguments; minWords 200.

SPEAKING: a.document = an original everyday advertisement (job, housing, course, service, leisure) with gaps of information the candidate must ask about; a.instruction (bilingual) tells the candidate to phone and ask about ten questions (5 minutes); a.partnerFacts = 10–15 facts the examiner/interlocutor knows and can reveal when asked (prices, times, conditions…). b.document = an original advertisement or announcement for an activity/opportunity; b.instruction tells the candidate to present it to a friend and convince them (10 minutes); b.partnerObjections = 6–10 realistic objections the friend raises.

Difficulty ranges from A2 to C1 across each paper, as in the real test. Plausible distractors; vary the correct option's position. Canadian and francophone-world contexts.
